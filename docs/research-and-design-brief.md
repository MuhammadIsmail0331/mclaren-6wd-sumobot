# McLaren 6WD sumobot pre-CAD brief

5 November 2026. Initial design study. The [Rev B build notes](../cad/rev-b/Build%20and%20shopping%20list.md) supersede its preliminary material and electrical assumptions.

**Screen a 250 × 250 mm assembled envelope** for the six selected motors. Use the 304.8 mm AIR NEXUS allowance if the measured hardware or a well-supported wedge needs the extra room. Keep the front module replaceable and test a 200 mm configuration separately. The 250 mm square is a team packaging target. Eligibility comes from each event's own size, mass and control rules.

The immediate competitive priority is a low entry edge that stays at a repeatable height while all six tires deliver usable traction. The simplest ramp candidate is a shallow entry with a steeper supported upper face, made from flat panels with a flush transition. Compare it with one flat wedge before committing to a smooth skin.

## 3. What the actual builds suggest

- **NaSCon 2026, team photos.** The black 6WD bot in the team’s October 2026 reference photos combines closely spaced wheels, a low rectangular main body and a broad straight scoop attached to a front wall. Its tires are exposed and its pack sits on top. Other frames show compact covered bodies and angled flank surfaces. The ring in those frames looks tiled or segmented, a reason to test the entry edge across seams. The stills establish geometry and visible tradeoffs. Match results, underside construction and exact dimensions are unavailable.
- **ProBot 6WD, user screenshots and product catalog.** The reference CAD is labelled 200 mm at the front and 198 mm along the side. It shows tight wheel spacing, a bolt-on straight wedge below a metal front wall and serviceable access to the internal hardware. The manufacturer's catalog describes a hybrid frame, steel base and stainless front wedge. Its compact dimensions depend on its Titan drive package. [ProBot product catalog](https://www.probotjo.com/products)
- **ProBot 8WD, user screenshots.** The build sequence shows motor carriers, a separate flat base, front and rear cross members, and a deck above the motors. This is a useful construction pattern for making separate pieces that can be printed or cut with local tools. The finished frame again shows substantial outboard wheel exposure. For McLaren, compare a removable guard against that exposed layout by looking at the measured width penalty and whether it keeps side hits off the hubs.
- **Curved scoop precedent.** Atlas documents a curved aluminum scoop on a printed chassis. A separate UniMAP prototype documents PETG with stainless steel. These show that hybrid and curved approaches are buildable in other classes. For this bot, performance needs a controlled comparison using the same lip height, wheel loading and test opponent. ## 4. First-pass packaging and fight mechanics

**Length.** With three equal tires in line, the tire train occupies `3D + 2g`, where D is cured OD and g is the loaded clearance between neighboring tires. At D = 57–58 mm and an illustrative g = 3 mm, the train is **177–180 mm**. The 200 mm class leaves **20–23 mm total** for everything extending beyond the tires at both ends. At 250 mm it leaves **70–73 mm**. These are screening values. The exact wedge projection depends on where the front axle and contact plane land.

**Width.** Opposed coaxial motors compete for the same center space. Put one real wheel and bracket on each motor. Measure A from the outside tire face to the farthest inboard motor terminal or wire bend. For symmetric aligned motors, the width starts at `2A + center gap + both guard allowances`. Even the supplier's 76.5 mm body length plus an illustrative 33 mm outboard stack would use 219 mm before the center gap and guards. Actual shaft/rim overlap can materially change that estimate. Test the paired layout on a full-scale paper outline before choosing a narrower chassis, staggered axles or a new drive arrangement.

**Mass.** Six motors at the supplier-listed 320 g are **1.92 kg**. If stock is steel near 7,850 kg/m³, a solid 250 mm square base at 1–1.5 mm would add approximately **0.49–0.74 kg** before cutouts. A 3 kg class would have limited room left for the wheels, battery, ramp, guards, electronics and fasteners. Weigh the actual parts and record a loaded axle balance before selecting ballast. The event's cap is a ceiling. The drivetrain's measured operating point should set the useful fighting mass.

**Push and steering.** Estimate usable straight push as the smaller of torque available at the wheels divided by loaded radius, and grip available from the driven normal loads. A straight pull/push test shows whether the bot spins or bogs. Repeatable turn tests show the steering cost of loading all six tires. A high-grip, long six-wheel footprint can increase lateral scrub during a pivot. Use tire contact and left/right current to decide whether axle-height shims or a shorter pitch improve control. An arbitrary drop at the middle axle risks unloading the outer wheels.

**Power.** The supplier's no-load 160 RPM implies about 0.49 m/s at 58 mm OD before load and voltage losses. Its listed 1.8 A no-load figure makes the current check particularly useful. The chat's earlier 6 A-per-motor stall estimate was used for four motors. Even if it applied to these units, scaling it to six would imply 18 A per side and 36 A total near stall, before other loads. Treat those numbers as a sensitivity check and log actual current, voltage sag and heat with the chosen battery. Avoid sustained locked-rotor testing. [Electrobes motor specs](https://electrobes.com/product/jgb37-550-dc-12v-160rpm-high-torque-metal-gear-dc-motor/) · [BTS7960 thermal and current limits](https://www.infineon.com/assets/row/public/documents/10/57/infineon-bts7960-ds-en.pdf)

**Ramp.** A low, shallow entrance starts engagement with lower horizontal effort. In an ideal frictionless, quasi-static model, pushing a vertical load up a local slope θ takes horizontal force proportional to `tan(θ)`. A steeper section increases lift per unit travel but demands more push and can stall at contact. A shallow entry and backed steeper face is therefore a hypothesis to test. Position the lip to pass below the opponent's edge while clearing the actual floor during turning, braking and tire compression. Check the load path through the front cross member and base. A rounded, deburred leading edge and the coordinator's ruling on clearance or floor contact are part of the decision.

## 5. Fabrication direction to evaluate

| Approach | Workshop requirement | What to test |
|---|---|---|
| Single straight ramp on side brackets | Flat laser-cut sheet, separate brackets or stock angles, accessible bolts | Baseline entry, contact force, stiffness and floor clearance |
| Two flat faces with a flush supported joint | Flat laser-cut sheets with a backing angle or internal ribs. A single straight brake bend is an option if available. | Compare with the baseline at the same lip height. Check snagging at the joint, concentrated loads and fastener heads. |
| Continuous sheet over curved ribs | Thin sheet cold-formed over a jig or retained against accurately shaped ribs. Verify springback, material and attachment with a coupon. | Check that the curve stays stable under load and remains smooth across its width. Adopt it if it improves engagement enough to justify fabrication time. |

Separate the load paths. Screen a metal floor and cross members with a metal front module for push and side loads. Use printed parts for battery/board trays, covers and locating features. PETG is a candidate for those parts if the actual filament and printer are available. The chat mentions PLA-CF and PETG-CF as future stock, so both need an availability check. A printed carrier can be considered after testing shaft alignment, layer orientation, fastening and impact loads. Bolt-on panels or guards make access to motors and electrical parts practical and avoid relying on welding thin unknown stock. Turn the side panel's lower edge and rear relief into measured clearance checks under pitch and tire deflection. A slope alone does not prevent an opponent's wedge from getting underneath. [Prusa PETG material guide](https://help.prusa3d.com/article/petg_2059)

If the shop offers straight bending, get its actual bend radius, tool clearance and minimum flange sizes before setting the SolidWorks sheet-metal rules and DXF flats. A broad printed body over the stated bed size would need to split into modules. [Fabricator guidance on bend limits](https://sendcutsend.com/guidelines/bend-deformation/)

## 6. What unlocks the SolidWorks layout

1. **Measure one assembled motor, mount and cured wheel.** Capture OD, width, total outer-face-to-terminal span, mounting hole pattern, boss and shaft engagement. Measure the actual plate and components on a scale. Use the battery and driver boards intended for the event, with connectors fitted.
2. **Confirm AIR NEXUS inspection details.** Get the category's exact slot, ring material and seams, how the complete envelope is measured, whether the passive scoop may lift an opponent without flipping it, and what leading-edge clearance/contact is acceptable.
3. **Bench-test the drivetrain before closing the enclosure.** Log total pack current/voltage and each side's current and driver temperature on starts, turns and representative pushing bouts. Measure tractive pull, then inspect wheel retention and shaft/bracket motion. Repeat after a short series of bouts to catch heating and sag. Use the result to size cooling and decide fighting mass.
4. **Model the validated parts, then prototype the nose.** Start SolidWorks 2025 with wheel/motor envelopes, three stations per side, battery/driver envelopes and an assembly size gauge. Once a layout clears those checks, model the metal load path, removable guards, printed trays and two interchangeable test ramps. Compare entry success and scraping on a representative surface and seam before issuing production DXFs and print files.

Prioritize fit and rule clarification in the first week, drive tests and a flat-wedge prototype in the second, fabrication and assembly in the third, and driving practice with spare hardware and inspection margin in the final week. This is a schedule anchored to the team's early-December window. The work here establishes the pre-CAD evidence and design choices. Release dimensions and fabrication files depend on the physical measurements and rule clarification above.
