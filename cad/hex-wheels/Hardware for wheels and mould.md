# Wheel revision hardware

This is additional wheel/tooling hardware. For chassis quantities see ../../docs/fasteners-and-supplies.md. The chassis and motor-holder geometry is unchanged.

| Item | Quantity | Use |
|---|---:|---|
| Brass wheel hex adapter, 6 mm motor bore, 12 mm hex, 18 mm overall | 6 | One on each motor; Electrobes 6 mm variant |
| M4 x 10 machine screw, socket cap or standard Phillips pan head, flat underside | 6 installed, buy 8 | Outer wheel retaining screw into brass; head at most 9 mm diameter and 4 mm high, fitting the 9.4 mm recess |
| M4 flat washer, 9 mm OD, about 4.4 mm hole, 1 mm thick | 12 | Six wheel-retaining washers and six mould bolt/nut washers |
| M4 x 4 cup-point grub screws | As many as the adapter's radial threaded holes, up to 12 for six adapters | Check the supplied adapter screws; no need to rebuy matching included screws |
| M4 x 20 low-head screw | 2 | Mould side lugs; these can be the two spares from the previous 20-screw order |
| M4 x 50 pan-head screw, head no more than 8.4 mm wide and 3.2 mm tall | 1 | Removable metal mould centre pin; fits recessed head under base |
| M4 plain hex nut, 7 mm across flats, about 3.2 mm thick | 3 | Two mould side bolts and centre pin |

All plastic holes are plain clearance holes. No printed M4 threads, heat-set inserts,
special spacers or precision printed motor-shaft fit are required for this revision.
The coupler's M4 female thread retains the wheel. Grub screws fasten the coupler to
the motor shaft, with a screw bearing on the shaft flat where available.

Do not substitute countersunk wheel screws. The model has a flat washer seat.
The M4 x 10 wheel screw and 1 mm washer give nominal 5 mm thread engagement in
the dimensioned reference. Check seating on the actual adapter, with no shaft or
screw bottoming. Tighten the coupler screws before sliding the wheel on; remove
the outer retaining screw and wheel to regain access. Use short grub screws so
the wheel can slide over the barrel without catching their ends.
